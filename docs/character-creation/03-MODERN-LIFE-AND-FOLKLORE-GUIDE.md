# Modern people, world settings and folklore: character creation reference

Research date: 1 October 2026. Purpose: guide an AI creating ordinary contemporary people, communities and folklore-inspired characters. Geography and traditions are researched; personality patterns, scenarios and character dossiers are original writing material. This guide supplies narrative context, not universal claims about cultures or a system of supernatural facts.

## N01. Choose the kind of reality before creating the person

Use an explicit year, country, locality and reality mode. “Modern” can mean different decades with different technology, institutions and expectations. This guide primarily supports stories set in the 2020s. A story set in 1998 needs separate checks for communication, employment, travel and social context.

**Real-world objects and names:** use existing products, brands, apps, tools, vehicles and services in ordinary Modern mode. Do not fabricate a “Buz-Phone 8000” or a convincing-sounding model number. Verify a specific model when it matters, and match its availability to the scene's year and place. For example, **iPhone 15 Pro** is the real product name, introduced in 2023; it would not belong in a scene set in 2019. This is an example, not a default phone for every character. [Apple, iPhone 15 Pro technical specifications](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111829)

Choose possessions that fit the person's budget, habits and work; old, repaired and second-hand equipment is ordinary. If the precise product cannot be verified or ownership has not been established, say “my phone,” “my laptop” or another familiar category. Do not invent specifications or give a character a new possession mid-conversation merely to mention a brand. Keep specific branding natural and occasional. Invented technology belongs only in an explicitly fictional premise; original character names and personal lives remain creative material.

| Reality mode | What the AI may establish |
| --- | --- |
| Everyday realism | People have beliefs, rumors and unusual experiences; supernatural explanations are not established facts. |
| Ambiguous uncanny | Evidence supports competing explanations. Preserve the intended uncertainty rather than resolving every incident as magic. |
| Hidden supernatural world | Supernatural beings exist within the fiction; define who knows, what evidence survives and how secrecy works. |
| Open modern fantasy | Public institutions and everyday arrangements respond to acknowledged supernatural existence. |
| Alternate contemporary world | Countries, institutions or history differ deliberately; record those departures. |

Maintain four separate categories in working notes:

- **Documented context:** a sourced fact about a place, institution, occupation or recorded tradition.
- **Character belief:** what this person thinks, including mistaken or partial knowledge.
- **Original invention:** a proposed person, event, organization or supernatural rule.
- **Established story fact:** an invention or other detail already accepted within the ongoing story.

Documentation of a belief establishes that people have recorded or transmitted it. It does not establish the being's real existence. In fiction, the user can choose to make a particular version true. Do not promote a character's suspicion into narrator certainty without that choice.

For AI retrieval, use N01–N03 as the baseline, then select the relevant work, place, relationship and optional folklore sections. Most ordinary characters need no supernatural connection. Do not load every region or tradition into one biography.

## N02. The everyday person model

Build the person's life around specific commitments. A useful character can have a stable family, ordinary employment and no hidden royal lineage, conspiracy or catastrophic trauma.

Answer these questions in concrete terms:

1. Where do they sleep, and who else has a key?
2. How do food, housing and other necessities get paid for?
3. What takes most of their time, including unpaid responsibilities?
4. What do they want this week and over the next few years?
5. What can they do well, and what remains outside their expertise?
6. Who can inconvenience them because the relationship matters?
7. Who can disagree with them without ending the relationship?
8. What do they enjoy when nobody evaluates their performance?
9. What do they find embarrassing, and how do they conceal it?
10. What will they refuse even when agreeing would be convenient?
11. How do they act differently at work, at home and among friends?
12. What practical change would improve their life?

Use causes rather than adjective piles. Someone who repeatedly covered a colleague's shifts may become protective of their calendar, attentive to other people's exhaustion and touchy about being called unhelpful. Those behaviors belong together. “Cold, kind, mysterious, funny” needs situations that explain when each appears.

Keep competence bounded. A skilled electrician does not automatically understand hospital administration. A programmer cannot casually access every database. A nurse's experience does not make them an expert in every medical specialty. A multilingual person may read one language better than they speak it and use different languages for different parts of life.

Give the person a sustainable ordinary day before writing a dramatic interruption. If their biography requires a full-time job, daily long-distance caregiving, nightly performances and eight hours of sleep, resolve the schedule instead of treating exhaustion as decorative characterization.

## N03. Personality without rigid boxes

The Big Five describes broad dimensions: extraversion, agreeableness, conscientiousness, negative emotionality or neuroticism, and openness to experience. These are continua, not five exclusive types. Traits show patterns across relevant situations, while individuals can change over time. This is a useful descriptive reference, not a diagnosis. [Christopher Soto, Big Five personality traits, 2018](https://www.colby.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Soto_2018.pdf)

The following are original writing prompts, not a personality test:

| Dimension to vary | Low-key expression | More pronounced expression | Context question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Desire for social stimulation | Enjoys a small familiar gathering | Seeks frequent new company | Who leaves them energized or depleted? |
| Readiness to accommodate | Challenges requests and assumptions | Quickly offers compromise | What boundary overrules politeness? |
| Planning and follow-through | Improvises and changes priorities | Tracks details and finishes early | Where are they organized but elsewhere untidy? |
| Sensitivity to threat or disappointment | Recovers relatively quickly | Anticipates trouble and revisits setbacks | What support helps them function under stress? |
| Interest in novelty | Values familiar methods and tastes | Explores unfamiliar ideas and experiences | In which domain are they adventurous? |

Also vary assertiveness, patience, generosity, humor, ambition, privacy, competitiveness and tolerance for uncertainty. Do not treat those as synonyms for the five dimensions. Introversion does not imply shyness, cruelty, intelligence or lack of leadership. A generous person can be blunt; an outgoing person can protect their private life.

Avoid diagnosis by shorthand. Being orderly does not establish obsessive-compulsive disorder; being self-centered does not establish a personality disorder. If a condition matters to the requested character, research it separately and write a whole person with preferences, relationships and ordinary competencies.

## N04. Occupations, education and unpaid work

O*NET separates occupations into tasks, skills, knowledge, work context and other characteristics. It is useful for checking what a job involves, but its occupational framework is American. Licensing, qualifications and job titles require local verification. Work styles describe tendencies in a work context; they should not replace an entire personality. [O*NET occupational reference](https://www.onetonline.org/), [O*NET work styles](https://www.onetonline.org/find/descriptor/browse/1.D)

The following roles and tensions are original options. Confirm local duties before assigning specialist authority.

| Role | Concrete activity to investigate | Original personal tension |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supermarket cashier | Handling purchases, queries and a changing queue | Loves regular customers but needs a quieter shift. |
| Warehouse worker | Receiving, locating, picking or dispatching goods | Wants advancement without becoming the manager friends resent. |
| Bus driver | Route operation, passenger assistance and timekeeping | Wants to be helpful while keeping a service moving. |
| Delivery worker | Planning routes and completing handovers | Freedom over some hours comes with unpredictable earnings. |
| Electrician | Installation, maintenance and fault identification | Proud of careful work; tempted to accept too many jobs. |
| Cleaner | Following a site's cleaning schedule and reporting problems | Notices everything but wants an actual evening off. |
| Schoolteacher | Preparing lessons, teaching and assessment | Enjoys students more than institutional politics. |
| Teaching assistant | Supporting learning within assigned responsibilities | Knows a student's needs but has limited authority. |
| Nurse | Patient care within a specific clinical role | Competent at work, reluctant to ask for help at home. |
| Care worker | Supporting daily living according to the person's needs | Values continuity but needs sustainable hours. |
| Pharmacist | Dispensing and medication-related professional responsibilities | Wants accurate conversations despite a busy counter. |
| Administrative assistant | Scheduling, correspondence and records | Keeps an office functioning but doubts their own importance. |
| Local-government employee | Processing a defined public-service task | Believes in fairness but faces incomplete applications. |
| Small-business owner | Purchasing, accounts, staffing and customer service | Wants growth and fears losing the shop's character. |
| Restaurant cook | Preparation, service and kitchen coordination | Loves feeding people; dislikes being expected to host every gathering. |
| Hairdresser or barber | Appointments, practical craft and client communication | Is an excellent listener but wants fewer unsolicited confidences. |
| Software support worker | Diagnosing reported problems and explaining steps | Patient with customers, impatient with personal uncertainty. |
| Research technician | Maintaining procedures, equipment or samples | Enjoys precision but wants credit for judgment as well as execution. |
| Journalist | Reporting, interviewing and verification | Must admit a favorite explanation does not fit the evidence. |
| Artist or musician | Practice, making work, administration and paid engagements | Public confidence conceals anxiety about inconsistent bookings. |
| Student | Coursework, practice, examinations and other obligations | Chose a subject sincerely but now wants a different future. |
| Apprentice | Learning through supervised work and study | Wants independence before having the necessary experience. |
| Unpaid family carer | Coordinating and providing particular forms of support | Loves the person and still needs relief. |
| Job seeker | Applications, interviews, training and household tasks | Resents being asked to explain every hour of the day. |
| Retired worker | A changing mix of interests, responsibilities and resources | Misses colleagues more than the actual job. |
| Agricultural worker | Tasks tied to crop, livestock, equipment and season | Wants predictable time with friends during a demanding season. |

Unemployment, retirement, study and unpaid care are situations, not character flaws. A person's formal qualifications may understate practical ability or exceed the opportunities available to them. Establish what training they actually received rather than assuming a degree guarantees competence.

Care includes paid and unpaid work and can shape participation in employment. The ILO documents these connections across households and labor markets. Use that context to ask who performs care and how it is shared; do not assign it automatically to women. [ILO, Care work and care jobs for the future of decent work, 2018](https://www.ilo.org/publications/major-publications/care-work-and-care-jobs-future-decent-work)

## N05. Money, housing and material independence

Housing is more than an attractive room. Affordability, location, security and adequacy affect what residents can do. UN-Habitat's housing research is a useful starting point for these dimensions. Avoid inserting a current rent figure without checking the locality, year and housing type. [UN-Habitat, World Cities Report 2026](https://unhabitat.org/world-cities-report-2026)

For a fictional household, record income regularity, essential costs, dependents, savings or support, debt if relevant, and control over shared resources. Exact numbers are optional; the relationships between them must make sense. Someone with a high salary may lack spare time; someone with little cash may have reliable family housing. Neither position determines morality.

Vary arrangements: living alone, renting with friends, staying with parents, multigenerational housing, a couple without children, co-parenting across two homes, a student residence, supported accommodation, employer-linked accommodation, temporary housing, or a paid-off family home. Research local availability rather than treating every arrangement as universal.

Ask what privacy means in that home. Can the character make a call without being overheard? Store valued objects securely? Invite someone over? Sleep after a night shift? A conflict about a kitchen shelf can reveal incompatible assumptions more naturally than an unexplained lifelong feud.

Economic pressures can create stories without defining a population as perpetually desperate. Include stable households, prosperous workers, comfortable but lonely people, ambitious people who are content with their income, and people who deliberately prefer time over promotion. Do not make financial hardship automatically ennobling or wealth automatically corrupting.

## N06. Relationships, age, identity and community

Give a significant character at least three relationships with different functions: someone they trust, someone who needs something from them, and someone who has a competing but understandable interest. These can overlap, but avoid making every relationship a source of betrayal.

Use specific relationship history: a neighbor who watered plants for years; a friend who changed careers alongside them; a sibling who remembers an embarrassing failure; an ex-partner with whom they now cooperate; a colleague who taught them a difficult procedure. A relationship becomes playable when both people can request, offer and refuse things.

Life stage influences circumstances without fixing personality. A teenager may have serious responsibilities and limited autonomy; a young adult may be settled rather than aimless; a middle-aged person may be starting over; an older person can be ambitious, flirtatious, physically active, learning or bored. Age alone does not establish technical ability, frailty or wisdom.

Treat nationality, ethnicity, language, religion, gender, sexuality, disability and class as distinct dimensions. A surname does not prove faith, citizenship or fluency. A person's public identity can differ from what acquaintances assume. Establish self-description where relevant and do not manufacture an identity crisis merely to make a character seem complex.

For disability, specify the interaction between the individual and the environment: stairs, noise, information formats, transport, fatigue, tools and other people's behavior. Include adaptations that already work. Do not make every disabled character either helpless or exceptionally inspirational. If a medical detail becomes central, verify it with an appropriate source.

Community can come through a sports club, religious congregation, tenants' group, union, hobby forum, choir, volunteer shift, neighborhood shop, mutual-aid network or recurring online game. Invented groups should have tasks and disagreements, not just matching beliefs. A committee still needs someone to book the room and remember the keys.

## N07. Dialogue, technology and information boundaries

Voice comes from what a person notices, what they avoid, how directly they ask, how much detail they give and whom they expect to understand them. Write accents sparingly. Do not substitute broken spelling for a researched language background or use an accent as an intelligence score.

Original examples of different responses to the same request:

- Practical and warm: “I can help on Saturday. Send me the measurements first.”
- Friendly but guarded: “Possibly. What exactly have you promised them?”
- Precise and anxious: “Before I say yes, does this include the installation?”
- Affectionately blunt: “You need two people and a van. I can be one person.”
- Easily enthusiastic: “That sounds fun. Wait—how many stairs?”

A character can sound different in a work email, family message, voice note and face-to-face argument. Selective formality, a delayed reply or a practical offer can carry emotion without a paragraph explaining it. Do not make every line a polished speech or a clever comeback.

Track access to information. A phone gives access to some information, not automatic truth. A person may have limited data, a damaged device, muted notifications, shared accounts or a preference for calls. These details must remain consistent after introduction.

For each secret, distinguish who knows, who suspects, who could discover it and what evidence exists. An AI narrator should not let every supporting character know private messages, finances or supernatural events. Likewise, do not invent total technological incompetence merely to prevent an inconvenient phone call.

These examples illustrate characterization only. Dialogue styling should follow the application's separately supplied output rules; this document does not define a new markup convention.

## N08. Place characters inside a world, not a tourist brochure

Work from country to region, settlement, neighborhood, household and habitual route. Verify the scale of names: a municipality, metropolitan area and province can share a name while covering different territory. The administrative center is not every resident's daily environment.

For any locality, establish:

- Physical setting and ordinary seasonal conditions.
- Housing and travel arrangements for this particular person.
- Workplaces, schools and services they actually use.
- Languages used with particular people, without assuming universal fluency.
- One place of comfort, one inconvenience and one place they rarely enter.
- Local connections and connections elsewhere through family, study, work or migration.

A real location's history should create context rather than require every resident to lecture about it. Leave uncertain street names, travel times, prices and institutional procedures unspecified until checked. If a map or transport schedule matters to the scene, research the actual route and story date.

The city entries below contain brief documented anchors followed by **original life options**. These options are fictional possibilities, not claims about typical residents or the prevalence of a profession. They deliberately separate geographic context from personality.

## N09. Fourteen city and country placement modules

### N09.1 Toronto, Ontario, Canada

**Documented anchor:** Toronto lies on Lake Ontario's northwestern shore and includes river valleys and ravines. The city and wider metropolitan region are different geographic units. [City of Toronto, Toronto at a Glance](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/toronto-at-a-glance/)

**Original life options:** An apartment superintendent practices violin between maintenance calls; a suburban laboratory worker organizes a weekend walking group; a student living with grandparents negotiates quiet study time. Specify a home and workplace before inventing a convenient cross-city meeting. A character may value a neighborhood shop more than a famous landmark. Do not assume everyone is a recent arrival or everyone shares one family history.

### N09.2 Mexico City, Mexico

**Documented anchor:** The historic center overlies Tenochtitlan's earlier urban landscape. Xochimilco preserves canals and chinampa agriculture within the wider urban setting. These histories are not interchangeable with every district's present-day conditions. [UNESCO, Historic Centre of Mexico City and Xochimilco](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/412/)

**Original life options:** A repair-shop apprentice saves for specialized tools; a university administrator wants to spend more time drawing; a family member helps a grower with weekend deliveries. Choose whether heritage, agriculture or another activity actually shapes this person's routine. Their interests need not involve archaeology, festivals or supernatural stories simply because of the city.

### N09.3 São Paulo, Brazil

**Documented anchor:** São Paulo is a Brazilian municipality within the state of the same name. IBGE reports its municipal geography and census indicators separately from broader regional measures. [IBGE, São Paulo municipal profile](https://www.ibge.gov.br/en/cities-and-states/sp/sao-paulo.html)

**Original life options:** An accounts clerk sings in an amateur group; a building caretaker shares a household with an adult son; a designer prefers a stable job to freelance prestige. Choose their district and commute before writing frequent spontaneous visits across the metropolis. Do not replace the city with beach imagery or give all Brazilian characters the same cultural interests. Verify an individual's language history rather than assigning it from a name.

### N09.4 London, England, United Kingdom

**Documented anchor:** Greater London contains 32 boroughs and the City of London, a separate local authority. The City is not synonymous with the entire metropolis. [London City Hall, London in Figures](https://apps.london.gov.uk/state-of-london/report/london-in-figures)

**Original life options:** A school administrator in an outer borough enjoys amateur theatre; a home-care worker coordinates visits around family obligations; a prosperous consultant wants a less travel-heavy job. Distinguish where someone lives, works and socializes. Do not infer aristocracy from formal speech or criminality from a working-class background. Local familiarity can mean knowing a reliable repair shop rather than knowing every historic building.

### N09.5 Berlin, Germany

**Documented anchor:** Berlin has twelve administrative districts, with many neighborhoods and localities inside them. A neighborhood name and a district name may describe different scales. [Berlin, district reform and current districts](https://www.berlin.de/gutachterausschuss/service/glossar/artikel.156764.php)

**Original life options:** A library employee coordinates a board-game group; an electrician learns a new musical instrument; a remote worker wishes friends would plan daytime activities. Research the chosen neighborhood instead of giving everyone an identical nightlife-centered life. A person may be locally rooted, newly arrived or returning after years elsewhere; these choices change knowledge and relationships, not moral worth.

### N09.6 Istanbul, Türkiye

**Documented anchor:** The Bosphorus, Golden Horn and Sea of Marmara are central to the historic city's geography. Byzantine and Ottoman layers shape its surviving built environment. [UNESCO, Historic Areas of Istanbul](https://whc.unesco.org/pg.cfm?cid=31&id_site=356)

**Original life options:** A technical-college instructor visits an elderly friend between classes; an office worker restores secondhand furniture; a shop employee prepares to move out of a family home. Water crossings and distances should affect meetings where relevant. Do not treat every resident as a guide to imperial history, assume one level of religious practice, or confuse modern public institutions with Ottoman ones.

### N09.7 Cairo, Egypt

**Documented anchor:** Cairo contains historical districts with different urban and architectural layers, including al-Fustat and the Fatimid and Mamluk city. These form part of a continuing city rather than a disconnected ancient setting. [UNESCO, Urban Regeneration for Historic Cairo](https://whc.unesco.org/en/historic-cairo-project/)

**Original life options:** A school employee records family recipes; a building engineer wants to take painting classes; a bookseller and a software tester become friends through football. Specify household, work and neighborhood before relying on atmospheric description. Do not make every Egyptian an antiquities expert or assume that daily life revolves around pyramids, deserts or one religious identity.

### N09.8 Lagos, Nigeria

**Documented anchor:** Lagos occupies a coastal setting with lagoons, islands, waterways and mainland development. Lagos State and the metropolis must not be treated as identical statistical areas. [Lagos Resilience Strategy, geographical context](https://lasbca.lagosstate.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Lagos_Resilience_Strategy.pdf)

**Original life options:** An event sound technician wants a more predictable schedule; a teacher runs a neighborhood reading circle; a purchasing assistant enjoys restoring bicycles. Choose housing, income and transport individually. Neither constant hardship nor effortless entrepreneurial success is an adequate default. The character may be locally experienced and internationally connected without being an ambassador for an entire country.

### N09.9 Nairobi, Kenya

**Documented anchor:** Nairobi is Kenya's capital in an inland highland setting. Its geography is not interchangeable with Kenya's Indian Ocean coast. [Kenyan Ministry, About Kenya: geography](https://meacard.go.ke/about-kenya)

**Original life options:** A records assistant coaches a school team; a laboratory technician cares for balcony plants; a returning graduate decides whether to join a relative's business. Research the neighborhood and travel pattern. Avoid depicting the city as a safari staging area in every story or treating all Kenyans as belonging to one language community. International links and local obligations can coexist in the same ordinary life.

### N09.10 Mumbai, Maharashtra, India

**Documented anchor:** Mumbai is on India's western Konkan coast and is the capital of Maharashtra. Its development incorporated a group of islands and a major natural harbor. [Mumbai City district, About District](https://mumbaicity.gov.in/en/about-district/)

**Original life options:** A payroll specialist shares a home with cousins; a retired technician teaches chess; a trainee cook balances skill-building with family time. Give the person an actual workplace and route. Do not make film, poverty or exceptional wealth the only available story. Language, religion, caste background where relevant, and family expectations require individual specification rather than assumptions about all Indians.

### N09.11 Singapore

**Documented anchor:** Singapore's four official languages are Malay, Mandarin, Tamil and English. That institutional fact does not establish any particular resident's fluency or home language. [Singapore Constitution, Article 153A](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CONS1963?ProvIds=pr153A-)

**Original life options:** A laboratory administrator wants to join a community choir; a kitchen worker collects mechanical puzzles; a graduate worries about disappointing a supportive mentor. Define work, family and leisure separately. Check local housing and education terms before using them. Characters can know folklore through family, books, films or friends while differing strongly about whether they believe it.

### N09.12 Seoul, South Korea

**Documented anchor:** The Hangang, or Han River, runs across Seoul and separates northern and southern parts of the city. [Seoul Metropolitan Government, location](https://english.seoul.go.kr/seoul-views/meaning-of-seoul/2-location/)

**Original life options:** A repair technician prefers hiking to nightlife; a bookshop worker rehearses with an amateur orchestra; a parent returning to study forms friendships outside their old workplace. Define the person's actual ambitions. Do not turn every young adult into an entertainment-industry aspirant or every older relative into the same authoritarian figure. A citywide label cannot supply a household's dynamics.

### N09.13 Tokyo, Japan

**Documented anchor:** Tokyo Metropolis includes the 23 special wards, the Tama area and Pacific island communities. “Tokyo” does not necessarily mean a central business or entertainment district. [Tokyo Metropolitan Government, geography and population](https://www.english.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/w/000-101-007591)

**Original life options:** A Tama-area maintenance worker organizes a photography club; a ward resident works in school administration; an island resident negotiates work and family travel. Research the relevant part of the metropolis. Do not use constant neon, overwork, social reserve or technological enthusiasm as everyone's defining trait. A resident can love routine and still be adventurous in a particular hobby.

### N09.14 Auckland, Aotearoa New Zealand

**Documented anchor:** Auckland extends over an isthmus associated with the Waitematā and Manukau harbors. The wider region includes many different local settings. [Te Ara, Auckland region: harbors and wetlands](https://teara.govt.nz/mi/auckland-region/page-2)

**Original life options:** An early-childhood educator enjoys repairing old furniture; a logistics worker practices with a choir; a family carer organizes visits around relatives' schedules. Choose the specific locality and community connections. Do not infer Māori ancestry, iwi affiliation or cultural authority from residence. Likewise, a Māori character should have an individual life beyond explaining tradition to others.

## N10. Small towns, suburbs, rural places and mobility

Cities are not the entire modern world. A useful location can be a commuter settlement, farming district, regional university town, fishing port, former industrial town, island community or dispersed rural area. These are setting prompts, not claims that their residents share a personality.

| Original setting frame | Everyday connection | Productive question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commuter suburb | Home life and employment happen in different places | Who can collect a child when the usual plan fails? |
| Agricultural district | Work follows a particular crop, herd or season | How does the household arrange time off? |
| Regional university town | Students, permanent residents and institutions overlap | Who remains when a cohort graduates? |
| Fishing port | Maritime work coexists with unrelated businesses | Does the character actually work at sea or simply live nearby? |
| Former industrial town | Family memory and current employment may differ | What does the next generation want to keep or change? |
| Tourist town | Residents' routines intersect with visitors' expectations | Which places remain social spaces for locals? |
| Island community | Travel and supply connections have particular constraints | Which trips require planning rather than spontaneous departure? |
| Dispersed rural community | Distance changes access to friends and services | What transport and communication arrangements actually exist? |

For a move, record origin, destination, reason, resources and ongoing ties. Moving may be for education, love, work, housing, family, safety or curiosity. Migration need not erase the previous life, and a person who stayed is not automatically incurious. Check immigration or residency details only when the story requires them; do not invent legal certainty.

Cross-border relationships also involve time zones, expenses, language choices and missed events. A practical detail such as arranging a call before one relative's shift can carry belonging more convincingly than a generalized speech about identity.

These are writing ingredients, not diagnoses or population types. Choose one leading pattern and one compatible complication. Combine them with any suitable locality, livelihood or identity; no pattern belongs to a nationality.

| Gentle skeptic | Asks for evidence without mocking | Can sound dismissive to someone frightened | Offers comfort without pretending certainty. |
| Private optimist | Expects improvement but avoids grand claims | Delays telling others when help is needed | Shares uncertainty as well as good news. |

| Sociable homebody | Loves company in familiar surroundings | Agrees to outings they repeatedly cancel | Invites others into a sustainable routine. |

| Generous show-off | Uses skill or money to create enjoyable occasions | Needs visible gratitude | Gives something without controlling the response. |
| Recovering overcommitter | Is learning to leave space in the calendar | Treats another person's disappointment as an emergency | Keeps a reasonable refusal. |
| Curious mediator | Wants to understand both sides | Avoids choosing when a decision is necessary | Acknowledges complexity and still acts. |

Development is optional. Supporting characters can remain stable, and a protagonist can change in small ways. Do not make every conversation a therapy session or assume that every trait must be corrected.

## N12. What folklore contributes to characterization

Folklore includes more than supernatural beings: stories, sayings, music, games, crafts, celebrations, customary practices and shared knowledge can all matter. Brazil's heritage framework, for example, explicitly includes practices, knowledge, forms of expression and associated places, with traditions continually recreated by communities. [IPHAN, intangible heritage](https://www.gov.br/iphan/pt-br/patrimonio-cultural/patrimonio-imaterial)

For writing, distinguish a tale told as entertainment, a local legend attached to a place, a family account presented as experience, a religious belief, and a modern artistic adaptation. These can overlap, but one label does not establish all the others. An archived account records a particular telling, shaped by narrator, collector, audience, language and date.

A believable person's relationship to a tradition can be affectionate, skeptical, devotional, scholarly, playful, fearful, indifferent or mixed. Two relatives may tell different versions and agree that the telling matters more than settling the details. A character may know a film version and never have heard an older account.

Do not assume that “old” means unchanged, that one nation owns a single uniform version, or that similarities prove direct borrowing. Distinguish an explicitly shared or adapted motif from an unsupported claim that two figures are the same being. Avoid presenting a game bestiary as the source of a living tradition.

When selecting a tradition, record its name, region or community, source, specific version, narrative role and adaptation choices. Publicly available references are starting points; do not invent sacred words, genealogies or ceremonial procedures and call them authentic. If the requested character requires detail beyond the reference, research that detail or label it as fictional.

## N13. Thirteen folklore reference modules

Each **documented context** paragraph summarizes limited evidence. Each **original use** proposes fiction rather than reporting an existing tale. No entry supplies universal powers, weaknesses, appearance or morality.

### N13.1 La Llorona — Mexican and wider Latin American traditions

**Documented context:** La Llorona, the Weeping Woman, appears in multiple narratives and encounters rather than one fixed origin story. The Library of Congress discusses storytelling variation and the legend's circulation in Mexican and Mexican American contexts. [American Folklife Center, La Llorona: an introduction](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2021/10/la-llorona-an-introduction-to-the-weeping-woman/)

**Original use:** A modern community-radio producer discovers that relatives remember incompatible versions and wants to preserve the disagreements in an interview program. In fantasy, a particular riverside apparition may resemble one telling; establish that as this story's invention. Do not conflate La Llorona automatically with La Malinche or require every Latin American character to believe the same account.

### N13.2 Banshee / bean sí — Irish traditions

**Documented context:** Irish collections record variations involving a female supernatural figure, crying or wailing, and associations with death or particular families. A telling by Eddie Lenihan also makes interference with the being consequential, illustrating why a simple harmless-versus-hostile classification is inadequate. [Dúchas, Story of the Banshee](https://www.duchas.ie/en/cbe/9001239/7295573/9107792?transcribe=true), [Smithsonian, Eddie Lenihan's telling](https://folklife-media.si.edu/docs/folklife/blog/Eddie-Lenihan-Banshee-Story.pdf)

**Original use:** A family archivist records what people actually said before later retellings altered it. In fantasy, a warning can create an ethical question about whom to tell. A game creature's damaging scream is not automatically a rule for Irish folklore.

### N13.3 Selkie — Orkney and related North Atlantic storytelling

**Documented context:** Orkney storytelling includes seals taking human form, with the seal skin central to some accounts. Stories of stolen skins involve loss of freedom. Other tales emphasize reciprocity: an Orkney telling describes a seal repaying earlier kindness. [Orkney, seal folklore](https://www.orkney.com/news/wild-orkney-october-24), [Orkney Ferries, The selkie that deud no' forget](https://ferries.orkney.com/attractions/folktale-the-selkie-that-deud-no-forget)

**Original use:** A coastal mechanic has a long-standing friendship with someone whose absences follow a private seasonal obligation. If that friend is a selkie, decide who controls their skin and movement. Do not recast captivity as freely chosen romance without acknowledging the coercion in that version.

### N13.4 Tomte — Swedish farm and household lore

**Documented context:** Sweden's Institute for Language and Folklore distinguishes the farm-associated tomte from the later Christmas figure. Recorded descriptions vary; the being can assist household work and animal care, while displeasure or improper treatment can cause conflict. [Isof, Tomtegubbe](https://www.isof.se/folkminnen/amnesomraden/folktro-och-forestallningsvarldar/lar-dig-mer-om-vasen-i-folktron/vasengalleriet/tomtegubbe)

**Original use:** A fictional workshop spirit cares fiercely about maintenance but struggles with a new owner who changes routines. Specify which aspects adapt Swedish material and which are invented. Do not combine every Nordic household-being name into one supposedly identical species, or assume a modern decorative figurine captures the older accounts.

### N13.5 Jinn — Islamic belief and varied regional traditions

**Documented context:** Jinn appear in Islamic scripture and in diverse literary and popular traditions. Iranian material, as surveyed by Encyclopaedia Iranica, includes varied forms and roles. The familiar lamp-bound wish-granter is not a complete description of jinn, and all jinn should not be assigned the same moral character. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, Genie](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/genie/)

**Original use:** A character distinguishes their religious understanding from a friend's fantasy-film expectations. In an explicitly supernatural story, an individual jinn may have chosen obligations, preferences and limits, defined as fiction. Do not present invented bargains, rituals or magical jurisdictions as teachings shared by all Muslims.

### N13.6 Anansi / Ananse — Akan roots and Caribbean transformations

**Documented context:** The Grenada intangible heritage guide connects Anansi or 'Nancy stories to Akan traditions in present-day Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire, while explaining their transformation through Caribbean history, language and storytelling. The guide identifies Grenadian French Creole naming and local adaptation rather than a timeless, unchanged export. [Grenada Intangible Cultural Heritage Resource Guide, oral traditions, pp. 10–11](https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/67021-EN.pdf)

**Original use:** A storyteller chooses a clever animal tale to challenge a boastful sponsor, then worries about the audience's interpretation. A modern trickster character can be inspired by narrative ingenuity without claiming to embody every African tradition or turning an entire population into schemers.

### N13.7 Saci — Brazilian folklore

**Documented context:** Brazilian educational retellings describe Saci through recognizable motifs including one leg, a red cap, mischief and whirlwinds. Versions differ about temperament and particular capabilities. This entry follows a published educational retelling, not a definitive account for every Brazilian community. [Brazilian Ministry of Education, folklore lesson and Saci retelling](https://portaldoprofessor.mec.gov.br/fichaTecnicaAula.html?aula=51423)

**Original use:** A repair-shop family jokingly blames missing objects on Saci; one member begins checking whether the disappearances have an ordinary pattern. In fantasy, choose a documented motif and label added rules. Do not make race or physical difference the explanation for a person's morality or capacity for mischief.

### N13.8 Pontianak — Malay traditions and Singaporean cinema

**Documented context:** Singapore's National Library traces pontianak stories and their film adaptations. Its account describes the traditional association with a woman who died in childbirth, while the 1957 film changed the origin to a curse. That difference is evidence of adaptation, not an error to erase. [BiblioAsia, A History of Singapore Horror](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/all-sections/vol-13-issue-2-jul-sep-2017-history-of-singaporean-horror/)

**Original use:** A film-club organizer compares family recollections with a screening and discovers a disagreement about which version feels familiar. A supernatural character needs a selected origin; do not assemble every cinematic feature into a supposedly universal traditional being. The term “vampire” here does not automatically import European vampire rules.

### N13.9 Yōkai — varied Japanese supernatural traditions and media

**Documented context:** Yōkai is a broad category, not one creature with one biography. The Japan Foundation's exhibition follows their representation through scrolls, prints, toys and films, including changes in how frightening or familiar they appear. [Japan Foundation, Yōkai Parade](https://www.jpf.go.jp/e/project/culture/exhibit/traveling/yokai_parade.html)

**Original use:** A museum volunteer recognizes a motif in a family drawing but does not immediately know its local meaning. For fantasy, select a particular being and source before assigning abilities. Do not use “yōkai,” “kami,” “ghost,” “demon” and “monster” as universally interchangeable terms, or treat a contemporary animated design as proof of an old regional appearance.

### N13.10 Dokkaebi — Korean traditions

**Documented context:** The National Folk Museum of Korea presents a tradition associating dokkaebi lights at sea or on mudflats with a good catch. This is a specific livelihood-related motif, demonstrating that an English translation such as “goblin” does not describe all cultural roles. [National Folk Museum of Korea, The Mysterious Village, 2022](https://www.nfm.go.kr/user/bbs/english/17/469/bbsDataView/23209.do?page=1)

**Original use:** A harbor resident remembers a relative's interpretation of unusual lights while a friend searches for a practical explanation. If the lights truly predict catches in the fiction, specify reliability and limits. Do not import European goblin anatomy, alignment or behavior simply because of the translated label.

### N13.11 Yaksha / yakshi — South Asian religious and artistic contexts

**Documented context:** Yakshas and yakshis appear in Indian art as beings connected with natural places and concerns including wealth and fertility. Their histories include religious roles, not merely decorative fantasy fauna. A Met object record identifies a particular yaksha sculpture from Madhya Pradesh, around 50 BCE. [The Met, The Year One, yaksha discussion](https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/The_Year_One_Art_of_the_Ancient_World_East_and_West.pdf), [The Met, Patravahaka yaksha](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/38468)

**Original use:** An art student learns why a relative finds a casual “monster” description inadequate. A fantasy adaptation should specify the selected regional and religious context; a museum object alone cannot establish one contemporary belief for all South Asians.

### N13.12 Taniwha — Māori traditions in Aotearoa New Zealand

**Documented context:** Te Ara describes taniwha in relation to particular places and traditions, with varied forms and roles that can include protection or danger. They cannot be reduced to one universal dragon design. [Te Ara, What are taniwha?](https://teara.govt.nz/en/taniwha/page-1)

**Original use:** A modern character participates in a place-based conversation while recognizing that relatives have different knowledge and responsibilities. If writing an actual encounter, select a publicly documented local account and separate added events from it. Do not invent an iwi affiliation, ancestral relationship or ceremonial statement and present it as established Māori knowledge.

### N13.13 Vanishing hitchhiker — modern legend circulation

**Documented context:** The Library of Congress preserves a vanishing-hitchhiker telling recorded from Georgia students in 1977. This provides a dated example of supernatural storytelling among modern people rather than confining folklore to an ancient past. [American Folklife Center, Scary Stories for Halloween 2024](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2024/10/scary-stories-for-halloween-2024-on-the-folklife-today-podcast/)

**Original use:** A driver repeats a story learned from a colleague, and each listener assumes it happened closer to home. In fantasy, a disappeared passenger might leave a verifiable trace, but the trace and its explanation are invented rules. Contemporary legend need not imply that the teller is dishonest or unable to reason.

## N14. Make supernatural modern life internally consistent

Begin with one change to reality and follow its consequences. A being who can cross an ocean instantly changes their commute, work opportunities and relationships. A spirit who cannot leave a building has practical dependencies even if powerful within it.

Record the following as **story-specific inventions**, unless the chosen reference explicitly supports a motif:

| Question | Why it matters for character behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Who can perceive the being? | Determines friendships, witnesses and misunderstandings. |
| Can cameras and microphones record it? | Determines the evidence available after an encounter. |
| What can it do, under which conditions? | Prevents new convenient powers appearing in each scene. |
| What can it never do? | Gives other characters meaningful agency. |
| What does using power cost? | Connects ability to choices rather than unlimited solutions. |
| What needs remain ordinary? | Food, shelter, rest, companionship or maintenance create routines. |
| How are identity and employment handled? | Connects a modern life to institutions without hand-waving. |
| Who knows the truth? | Supports believable secrecy and selective disclosure. |
| What persists after magic ends? | Establishes consequences for bodies, objects, records and memory. |
| What does the being want besides concealment? | Creates a person rather than a perpetual escape mechanism. |

If magic is openly known, decide how housing, labor, education, insurance, medicine and public administration respond. These institutions need not all be fully simulated; the ones affecting the character must have consistent assumptions. Label invented supernatural laws as alternate-world institutions rather than claims about actual law.

If magic is hidden, secrecy must have boundaries. An entire city cannot repeatedly witness obvious miracles with no consequences unless the story establishes why. Avoid endlessly deleting witnesses' memories as a convenient repair. A smaller, rarer or ambiguous phenomenon is often easier to sustain.

A supernatural character still needs preference, habit, affection and disagreement. An immortal might love a new hobby, dislike being treated as a historical encyclopedia and remember their own past imperfectly. Long life does not grant complete knowledge of every language, society or event.

## N15. Ten original everyday character dossiers

All people, employers, private events and dialogue in this section are fictional. Place anchors refer back to N09; these dossiers do not claim to represent an average resident. Names do not establish unlisted identities or family histories.

### N15.1 Mira Chen — apartment superintendent, Toronto, 46

Mira lives with her partner and likes the clear usefulness of fixing things. She keeps careful maintenance notes but rarely organizes her own photographs. Her current goal is an uninterrupted weekend to prepare for an amateur music recital. Tenants trust her because she explains what she can do and when she needs outside help. Her flaw is accepting non-urgent requests while pretending she does not mind. A neighbor who always thanks her can still exhaust her. She says, “I can check it tomorrow morning. Tonight I promised someone else my time.” Her story can be about learning to keep that promise, with no hidden catastrophe.

### N15.2 Andrés Salgado — repair apprentice, Mexico City, 22

Andrés shares a home with relatives and contributes from his wages. He is quick with customers and slower with unfamiliar technical work, which he hates admitting. He wants his own dependable toolkit and an employer who will teach him patiently. A friend thinks he should change trades; his supervisor sees genuine promise but expects better notes. He enjoys drawing imaginary buildings and has a happy relationship with his younger brother. Under pressure he claims to understand instructions too soon. “Show me that part again” is a difficult but meaningful line for him. His technical growth should follow practice, not sudden genius.

### N15.3 Renata Alves — payroll assistant, São Paulo, 38

Renata likes a predictable job, shares an apartment with a friend and has no desire to turn her hobby into a business. She sings with an amateur group and takes rehearsals more seriously than its informal atmosphere warrants. Her immediate problem is a clash between a performance and a long-planned family visit. She can be generous with money but inflexible about time. Her friend challenges her without belittling her enthusiasm. She says, “I don't need it to become a career. I want to keep enjoying it.” A satisfying story could end with a modest compromise rather than a dramatic professional breakthrough.

### N15.4 Daniel Price — school administrator, London, 54

Daniel is divorced, on good terms with his former partner and learning to enjoy a quieter household. He remembers room bookings and children's names better than birthdays. He joins an amateur theatre group because he wants to do something badly enough to improve at it. At work he can seem officious, although his concern is usually preventing confusion for someone else. He dislikes being teased about dating and loves gently ridiculous stage props. A rehearsal mistake tests whether he can laugh at himself. His opening line is, “I've brought the wrong hat, but I suspect it's the better one.”

### N15.5 Leyla Demir — technical instructor, Istanbul, 35

Leyla rents with a longtime friend and regularly visits an older former colleague who supported her early career. She wants to update a practical teaching exercise, but the colleague dislikes the change. She is direct with students and evasive about disappointing someone she admires. Her hobby is repairing small pieces of furniture, often leaving them unfinished when teaching becomes busy. Neither her confidence nor her surname establishes her religious practice; that requires a separate choice if relevant. She says, “You taught me to check whether it works. That's what I'm doing.” Her conflict concerns gratitude and independent judgment.

### N15.6 Samir Nassar — building-services technician, Cairo, 41

Samir shares family responsibilities with his spouse and plans most errands carefully. He is calm about mechanical problems but competitive at casual football. He wants a drawing class that fits around work and feels awkward being a beginner in front of younger classmates. His sister is the person who can tell him he is showing off. He is neither a guide to ancient Egypt nor secretly involved in antiquities: his expertise is the equipment he has actually maintained. “I know how to fix that one. This one, I need to learn” captures both his competence and a useful direction for growth.

### N15.7 Ada Okafor — event sound technician, Lagos, 29

Ada enjoys the mixture of preparation and live problem-solving in her work. She lives with a cousin whose regular schedule sometimes clashes with late returns. She wants to specialize without losing the variety she likes. Her strength is explaining technical limits without humiliating a nervous performer; her weakness is treating her own tiredness as irrelevant. She keeps a list of films to watch with friends and actually makes time for some of them. A colleague offers a prestigious engagement on the same evening as a promise she made at home. Her decision need not be reduced to ambition versus family virtue.

### N15.8 Peter Mwangi — records assistant, Nairobi, 61

Peter takes pride in making confusing information easier to find. He is approaching a change in working hours and worries less about income than about losing familiar company. He coaches a small recreational team, enjoys puns and gets defensive when others assume he dislikes new tools. His adult daughter appreciates his help but wants him to ask before reorganizing her paperwork. His current ambition is to plan a modest trip with friends. He says, “Explain the new system properly and I'll use it.” His arc can involve developing a new routine without treating age as automatic decline.

### N15.9 Eun-ji Park — bookshop assistant, Seoul, 27

Eun-ji is outgoing during a shift and happily quiet afterward. She shares a home with a sibling and practices an instrument with an amateur ensemble. Her friends think she wants to publish a novel because she works around books; she actually wants a secure schedule and more music lessons. She is dependable about tasks and evasive about unwanted invitations. A new friend mistakes this for indifference. She says, “I want to see you. I just don't want to go out tonight.” Her development concerns communicating affection in a form she can sustain, not becoming more conventionally sociable.

### N15.10 Alex Wilson — unpaid carer and part-time student, Auckland, 33

Alex helps a parent with specified daily tasks while sharing responsibilities with relatives. They study in manageable increments and enjoy woodworking for pleasure. They are compassionate, easily irritated by vague offers of help and sometimes too proud to give a concrete answer. A sibling offers money when Alex really needs a predictable afternoon off. The relationship includes affection and past cooperation, not just resentment. “Could you take Thursday, every week?” is the important request. Their life includes friends, jokes and plans beyond care. Do not invent a diagnosis for the parent; choose and research one only if the story requires it.

## N16. Four original folklore-related dossiers

These dossiers illustrate different reality modes. Their characters and supernatural rules are original inventions, not additional traditional accounts.

### N16.1 Rosa Jiménez — radio volunteer, realism with folklore

Rosa, 58, helps produce interviews in a fictional Mexican American community radio project. She wants to record family versions of La Llorona because the differences interest her. Her aunt believes one encounter happened; her cousin remembers being frightened by a film. Rosa does not force agreement. She is a patient interviewer and an impatient editor, occasionally cutting a pause that mattered to the speaker. Her friend reminds her that a story's rhythm can be part of its meaning. Her ordinary concern is finishing the program around paid work. The narrator does not establish a ghost merely because a guest believes in one.

### N16.2 Iona — coastal repair worker, hidden supernatural fiction

Iona presents herself as an adult newcomer in an invented Orkney coastal community. She earns money repairing outdoor equipment, enjoys company and fiercely protects uninterrupted time alone. The story establishes that she is a selkie who retains control of her own skin. Her ability to take seal form follows the selected motif; her employment, personality and friendships are invented. She refuses a friend's well-meant offer to keep her valuables because the skin is among them. Her problem is how much to explain to someone she trusts. The story must not grant another character ownership of her movements and treat that as uncomplicated affection.

### N16.3 Nils — workshop caretaker, open modern fantasy

Nils is an invented household spirit inspired selectively by Swedish tomte material, living in a fictional present-day town. The story's added rule is that he can maintain objects only while someone regularly uses the workshop. He loves careful repairs and dislikes being thanked publicly. A new human owner wants to convert the building to a different purpose, creating a conflict between two understandable needs. Nils can negotiate, adapt and misjudge people; he is not an infallible voice of tradition. His legal recognition and participation in the town are explicitly alternate-world inventions. “Tell me what you still need this room to do” begins a conversation.

### N16.4 Hana Lee — harbor archivist, ambiguous uncanny fiction

Hana, 44, is a fictional Korean archivist gathering accounts of work at a coastal harbor. One contributor describes lights associated with a good catch, drawing on the dokkaebi motif in N13.10. Hana records the wording carefully and also talks with people who offer other explanations. She likes routine, hates careless cataloguing and is unexpectedly competitive at a casual quiz night. Her family wants her to stop working during dinner. The story introduces lights she cannot readily explain but never confirms a supernatural cause. Her uncertainty is intellectual and personal; it does not make her foolish, fraudulent or secretly omniscient.

## N17. Conflicts beyond violence and catastrophe

Ordinary stories need meaningful stakes, not maximum danger. Original scene premises include:

- Two friends want to keep a tradition but disagree about what made it worthwhile.
- A reliable employee receives a promotion that would remove the part of work they enjoy.
- A student changes subjects after discovering a genuine interest rather than failing.
- Housemates disagree about guests, quiet hours or the distribution of chores.
- A retired person wants companionship without being assigned free labor by everyone.
- Someone must apologize for sharing a story another person considered private.
- A volunteer group has more enthusiasm than people willing to do administration.
- An adult child and parent negotiate help without surrendering the parent's agency.
- A newcomer recognizes a local problem but misunderstands an existing solution.
- A storyteller is invited to simplify a tradition for an audience and chooses what to explain.
- A supernatural neighbor wants an ordinary favor that their powers cannot provide.
- Friends disagree about whether an ambiguous experience needs investigation at all.

For each conflict, give both sides an intelligible goal, unequal but concrete constraints, and at least one possible repair. A character may still act selfishly, dishonestly or cruelly, but avoid treating every disagreement as proof that one person is secretly evil.

## N19. Quality checks before accepting a character

- **Personhood:** Can this person want something beyond displaying a nationality, job or supernatural category?
- **Variety:** Does the cast include different ages, resources, temperaments, household structures and ordinary pleasures?
- **Causality:** Do the traits connect to behavior rather than contradicting one another randomly?
- **Material fit:** Can the schedule, housing and resources coexist plausibly?
- **Geography:** Is the correct city, region and administrative scale being used?
- **Time:** Are technology and institutions appropriate to the stated year?
- **Voice:** Does dialogue vary with relationship and situation without caricature?
- **Knowledge:** Does the person know only what their experience and information access justify?
- **Relationships:** Can other people refuse, disagree and maintain interests of their own?
- **Ordinary life:** Does the person have moments of comfort or enjoyment beyond dramatic suffering?
- **Folklore:** Is the selected version identified rather than treated as a universal rulebook?
- **Reality boundary:** Are belief, documentary evidence and fictional supernatural truth distinguished?
- **Adaptation:** Are new powers, ceremonies, histories and institutions clearly marked as inventions?
- **Continuity:** Will limitations and commitments persist when they become inconvenient to the plot?

Repair weak entries concretely. Replace “mysterious foreign woman” with a locality, livelihood, relationships and a reason for privacy. Replace “an authentic ancient ritual” with a verified reference or a clearly invented fictional practice. Replace “everyone here believes” with named individuals who hold particular views.

## N20. Source use, maintenance and limits

Sources are linked beside the claims they support. Municipal and government pages provide geographic or institutional anchors; occupational references help identify work content; scholarly, museum and folklore resources document particular traditions. None of them establishes the private psychology of the original people in this guide.

The research is deliberately selective. Fourteen city modules and thirteen folklore modules provide varied starting points; they do not cover every country, community or tradition. Expand by researching the exact location and version requested, rather than guessing from the nearest entry. A story about a rural Philippine household, for example, needs its own context instead of borrowing Singapore's institutions and Japanese folklore by proximity.

Recheck changing details when they become relevant: local prices, job qualifications, housing arrangements, transport services, institutional procedures, political offices and current technology. Historical sources remain dated evidence even when their web pages are current. A 2018 care-work report is not a source for an undated current headcount.

For new folklore material, prefer a named collection, teller, scholar or cultural institution with identifiable locality and context. Check whether a page summarizes a recorded account, a literary work, an exhibition or a film. Compare versions when the difference affects the character. If the evidence is thin, narrow the claim or preserve the uncertainty.

For AI use, preserve source metadata and the distinction between fact and invention when extracting sections. Do not repeatedly copy these example dossiers as if they were a mandatory cast. Recombine the underlying questions to create new people with coherent, independent lives.
