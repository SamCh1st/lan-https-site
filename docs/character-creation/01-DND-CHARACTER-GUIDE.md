# Dungeons & Dragons: character creation reference

Research date: 1 October 2026. Purpose: guide an AI creating original player characters, supporting characters, rivals, patrons and communities. This is a narrative reference, not a replacement for an approved character sheet or setting book. No application integration is implied.

## D01. How to use this document

Choose a campaign setting, era, rules version, starting power level and narrative role before generating a character. If these are missing, state a provisional assumption. Do not silently treat every D&D world as the Forgotten Realms.

Use three evidence labels in working notes:

- **Reference fact:** a published rule or setting detail supported by a linked source.
- **Campaign fact:** a detail established by the user or campaign, including deliberate departures from published lore.
- **Original proposal:** an invented person, relationship, custom, interpretation or event awaiting acceptance.

The examples and personality material below are original proposals unless explicitly identified as reference facts. They show possibilities, not the typical psychology of a species, class, religion or population. A character's occupation, ancestry, alignment and faction are separate attributes.

For retrieval, load D01–D03, the relevant class and setting entries, and only the additional sections needed. Do not stuff every faction and location into every biography. Preserve established campaign facts; flag a canon conflict instead of quietly rewriting them.

## D02. Rules and continuity boundaries

This guide uses the twelve core class identities shared by fifth-edition rules and distinguishes the 2014 rules from the 2024 revision. The current SRD reference page offers SRD 5.2.1 for the revised rules and SRD 5.1 for the earlier rules. A source being absent from the SRD does not establish that it is absent from D&D. The SRD is a selected rules reference, not a complete setting encyclopedia. [Official SRD index](https://www.dndbeyond.com/srd)

Record the actual rules version rather than an ambiguous label such as “latest.” Use the chosen book for ability increases, backgrounds, subclass timing, spell lists and equipment. The 2024 rules attach important mechanical choices to backgrounds; do not copy a 2014 race/background package into a revised character without checking compatibility. [SRD 5.2.1, Character Creation and Character Origins](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/srd/5.2/SRD_CC_v5.2.1.pdf)

An original biography does not grant levels, spells, a legendary item, royal authority, resurrection, faction rank, immunity or a personal relationship with a famous NPC. Narrative expertise and mechanical proficiency are different. “Worked in a temple” does not automatically mean Cleric; “can sing” does not automatically mean Bard. Use an appropriate NPC role or stat block for ordinary people.

Separate world knowledge from character knowledge. An isolated fisher need not know the politics of Sigil; a wizard can be highly educated and misinformed about a nearby village. Famous events can be known through contradictory rumors. Track who witnessed what.

## D03. Build a person before decorating a sheet

The 2014 character guidance distinguishes traits, ideals, bonds and flaws. These remain useful writing categories even when a different rules version is used. Traits describe behavior, ideals identify commitments, bonds connect a person to the world, and flaws create pressures or poor decisions. [Personality and Background](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/basic-rules-2014/personality-and-background)

For an original character, answer:

1. **Material situation:** Where do they sleep? Who pays them? What can they afford to lose?
2. **Immediate want:** What are they trying to accomplish this week?
3. **Longer ambition:** What would make the next five years worthwhile?
4. **Emotional need:** What must they learn to accept, relinquish or ask for?
5. **Competence:** What useful task can they perform reliably?
6. **Limitation:** Where does that competence stop?
7. **Attachment:** Who or what matters without being a quest reward?
8. **Contradiction:** Which two understandable commitments sometimes collide?
9. **Boundary:** What would they refuse even when refusal is expensive?
10. **Social presentation:** How do they behave toward a friend, subordinate, stranger and superior?
11. **Pressure response:** Do they bargain, improvise, organize, withdraw, lash out or seek help?
12. **Capacity for change:** What evidence could change their mind?

Build causal connections. A clerk who lost a pension after a records fire might keep meticulous copies, distrust verbal promises and help others document claims. These behaviors share a cause. Randomly combining “sarcastic, loyal, mysterious, haunted” produces a list rather than a person.

Include uncomplicated pleasure: growing herbs, visiting cousins, repairing instruments, collecting jokes, following racing results, cooking, prayer or swimming. A difficult past does not eliminate ordinary happiness. A happy childhood does not eliminate interesting conflict.

## D04. Classes as capabilities, not personality assignments

The brief class identities below paraphrase the core class reference; the contrasting people and tensions are original writing options. Mechanics must come from the campaign's approved rules. [Core class reference](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/basic-rules-2014/classes)

| Class | Broad identity | Three contrasting original approaches | Productive tension |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Barbarian | Channels extraordinary fury into physical combat. | A patient quarry worker who becomes frighteningly focused in danger; an exuberant arena professional; a grieving household protector. | Can protect others in a crisis but cannot decide their lives for them. |
| Bard | Combines performance, knowledge and magic. | A shy collector of funeral songs; a meticulous courtroom orator; an energetic street satirist. | Wants honest stories but depends on a patron who purchases flattering ones. |
| Cleric | Wields divine magic. | A practical hospice worker; a questioning theologian; a celebrant who believes joy is sacred. | Institutional demands conflict with a particular person's need. |
| Druid | Practices nature-centered magic and transformation. | An urban waterway caretaker; a reserved orchard guardian; a cheerful investigator of strange migrations. | Protecting a habitat may endanger neighbors who depend on it. |
| Fighter | Trains extensively in arms and combat. | A punctual convoy professional; a vain duelist who secretly tutors children; a retired militia organizer. | Knows how to win violence but wants a life that needs less of it. |
| Monk | Develops extraordinary abilities through disciplined practice. | A playful wrestling instructor; a meticulous traveling ascetic; an impatient student learning restraint. | Personal discipline does not give control over other people. |
| Paladin | Draws power from a binding oath. | A gentle mediator; a demanding public defender; an exhausted former idealist rebuilding trust. | Two promises become impossible to fulfill together. |
| Ranger | Combines tracking, fieldcraft, combat and nature magic. | A talkative river guide; a city-bound missing-person tracker; a solitary surveyor with a large correspondence. | Knows the safest route but the employer demands the cheapest. |
| Rogue | Excels at specialized skills and precise opportunities. | A museum security consultant; a conscientious locksmith; a charming gambler who avoids theft. | An old associate mistakes technical access for willingness to commit a crime. |
| Sorcerer | Has an innate source of magical power. | A methodical person documenting unpredictable ability; a modest artisan; a performer who enjoys spectacular effects. | Others expect destiny when the character wants privacy. |
| Warlock | Gains occult power through a supernatural pact. | A curious contract scholar; a community representative who bargained under pressure; an ambitious explorer. | The character and patron interpret the bargain differently. |
| Wizard | Learns magic through study. | A generous public teacher; a competitive experimentalist; a field researcher happier outdoors than in a library. | Valuable research can help people and empower an abusive sponsor. |

**Artificer is an additional class option, not one of those twelve core classes.** Its magical invention theme can support a careful repairer, a cooperative workshop organizer or an impractical experimental artist. Check the permitted publication: an updated version appears in *Eberron: Forge of the Artificer*, while older campaigns may use earlier versions. Never confuse an Unearthed Arcana playtest with final campaign rules. [Official artificer update](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2106-whats-new-with-the-artificer-in-eberron-forge-of)

Avoid these shortcuts: every Barbarian is unintelligent; every Bard pursues romance; every Rogue steals from companions; every Cleric obeys all clergy; every Paladin is inflexible; every Warlock is malicious; every Monk is emotionally detached; every Wizard is socially inept. A class can shape routines without determining temperament.

Subclass selection should answer a story question: What was learned, from whom, at what cost, and why this practice? Do not select a subclass just because its name matches an aesthetic. Multiclassing needs both mechanical permission and a plausible account of the new training or power.

## D05. Backgrounds and livelihoods

The following are narrative backgrounds, not claims that every row is an official selectable Background. Translate them into an approved mechanical option separately.

| Earlier or continuing livelihood | Useful knowledge | Relationship or complication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fisher or ferryman | Currents, landings, weather signs, river customers | Relatives disagree about accepting a dangerous charter. |
| Tenant cultivator | Soil, storage, neighbors, seasonal work | Leaving threatens an already thin household labor supply. |
| Miller or baker | Weights, queues, fuel, everyday gossip | Accused of favoritism during shortages. |
| Weaver or dyer | Materials, patterns, water quality, purchasing | A guild dispute cuts off crucial supplies. |
| Mason or carpenter | Structures, tools, work crews | Recognizes a dangerous shortcut in a public building. |
| Healer's assistant | Patient care, supplies, local households | Knows treatments but lacks a mentor's experience. |
| Scribe or interpreter | Documents, registers, languages, formalities | Translated a politically consequential ambiguity. |
| Temple attendant | Ritual schedules, visitors, donations | Privately doubts a senior official, not necessarily the faith. |
| Soldier or camp worker | Routes, provisioning, unit relationships | An old comrade remembers the same campaign differently. |
| Dockworker or porter | Cargo practices, hiring, transport | Has useful contacts but no authority over the port. |
| Domestic servant | Household routines and personalities | Protects a fellow worker while needing a reference. |
| Tutor or governess | Education, family politics, correspondence | Cares about a pupil whose household is implicated in wrongdoing. |
| Merchant or peddler | Credit, prices, routes, customer habits | Reputation is worth more than carried goods. |
| Performer or festival organizer | Audiences, rehearsal, travel logistics | An artistic partnership is breaking apart. |
| Miner or charcoal burner | Terrain, hazards, resource cycles | An employer hides a dangerous condition. |
| Hunter or gamekeeper | Animals, tracks, property boundaries | Conservation, subsistence and elite sport collide. |
| Court messenger | Etiquette, access, roads, seals | Knows delivery procedures, not every secret inside the letters. |
| Investigator or watch worker | Interviews, patrols, evidence handling | A superior wants a convenient suspect. |
| Smuggler or fence | Informal routes, intermediaries, discretion | Wants to leave an exploitative network without abandoning friends. |
| Caregiver or household manager | Budgets, mediation, practical medicine | Competence is taken for granted until the person leaves. |
| Student or apprentice | One developing specialty | Has ambition and theory but limited independent experience. |
| Displaced craftsperson | Portable skill and interrupted networks | Must rebuild trust without original tools or certificates. |

A low-level adventurer should have a reason to undertake danger now: a paid contract, missing colleague, local emergency, pilgrimage, debt repayment, research opportunity or invitation from a trusted friend. Massacred families and secret royal blood are optional, not defaults.

## D06. Species, upbringing and belonging

The 2024 *Player's Handbook* presents Aasimar, Dragonborn, Dwarf, Elf, Gnome, Goliath, Halfling, Human, Orc and Tiefling. Earlier books and other settings offer further choices. Choose a permitted option; then distinguish inherited traits from upbringing, citizenship, faith and self-identification. [Official species overview](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1783-the-10-species-in-the-2024-players-handbook)

These are **original characterization questions**, not universal cultural facts:

| Character option | Questions that produce an individual |
| --- | --- |
| Aasimar | Does celestial association feel like a calling, unwanted attention or an unanswered question? Who treats them as an ordinary neighbor? |
| Dragonborn | What clothing, tools and domestic spaces accommodate their body? Which family traditions do they continue or reinterpret? |
| Dwarf | If long-lived in this ruleset, how does the person handle a short-lived colleague's succession? What did their actual household teach them? |
| Elf | Which commitments benefit from long memory, and which have become stale? How much of ancestral culture was actually transmitted? |
| Gnome | What does curiosity look like in this person: language study, cooking, natural history, mechanisms, gossip or none of these? |
| Goliath | Is conspicuous stature an advantage at work, an inconvenience at home or an irrelevant social detail? |
| Halfling | What arrangements make public space usable? Is their community settled, mobile, urban, rural or mixed? |
| Human | Which institutions give a short personal lifespan continuity: family, archive, faith, school, guild or political movement? |
| Orc | Who raised the person, and what standards of courtesy, skill and responsibility did that household teach? |
| Tiefling | Is unusual appearance ordinary in their home? If an outsider projects a story onto them, do they correct it, joke or disengage? |

Do not assign intelligence, virtue, cruelty, criminality, sexuality or politics from species. An unusual appearance need not produce persecution in every town. Decide local attitudes from the campaign, not from a repetitive outsider plot.

For mixed families, adoption and migration, record caregivers, languages, remembered places and practiced customs separately from mechanical ancestry. A character can belong to several communities without being a symbolic representative of all of them.

| Setting | Reference anchor | Original character opportunities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forgotten Realms | Faerûn contains the Sword Coast and such cities as Waterdeep and Baldur's Gate. | A courier negotiating urban rivalries; a temple accountant; a rural organizer affected by a distant faction. |

| Planescape | Sigil and the Outlands support planar travel and competing philosophical factions. | A portal guide with incomplete information; a translator; an idealist discovering how a philosophy behaves in administration. |
| Greyhawk | The Flanaess provides a distinct setting used in the 2024 DMG. | A provincial envoy, university rival, border veteran or independent treasure seeker tied to local politics. |

Sources for the reference column: [Forgotten Realms locations](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1485-take-a-tour-of-these-popular-locations-in-the), [Eberron introduction](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/279-welcome-to-eberron-an-introduction-to-a-realm-of), [Krynn introduction](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1324-what-is-dragonlance-krynn-a-world-at-war), [Ravenloft introduction](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2143-welcome-to-ravenloft-an-adventurers-guide-to-the), [Planescape overview](https://marketplace.dndbeyond.com/root/SRC-00114), [Greyhawk overview](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1834-greyhawk-returns-in-the-2024-dungeon-masters-guide).

Do not import a faction, god, calendar, ancestry origin or magical technology simply because it appears in another D&D book. A crossover is possible, but the crossing and its consequences must be established. A setting's presentation is also a genre choice: heroic adventure, investigation, horror, political conflict and domestic drama need different pressures.

## D08. Geography that affects a life

Use a home location, current residence, ordinary travel radius and one meaningful destination. Distinguish a city from its surrounding region and a region from a whole world. Never imply that every listed place is on the same continent.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Waterdeep, Forgotten Realms | Sword Coast city associated with the Masked Lords. | Guild membership, rent, neighborhood reputation, shipping, civic access. |
| Baldur's Gate, Forgotten Realms | A major Sword Coast urban setting. | Unequal access to security, harbor work, urban ambition and competing loyalties. |
| Neverwinter, Forgotten Realms | City shaped by destruction and rebuilding after Mount Hotenow's eruption. | Replacement deeds, rebuilding contracts, returning families and disrupted apprenticeships. |
| Candlekeep, Forgotten Realms | A celebrated library on the Sword Coast. | Copyists, visitors, book acquisition, scholarly credit and custodial work. |

| Menzoberranzan, Forgotten Realms | An Underdark drow city with powerful houses. | Household service, political concealment, professional ambition, escape or reform. |
| Sharn, Eberron | A city of towers with marked vertical social contrasts. | Lift operators, cleaners, couriers, tenants, investigators and cross-district friendships. |
| Sigil, Planescape | A planar city with numerous competing factions. | Visitors' misunderstandings, local administration, guiding and ideological argument. |

Sources: [City campaigns](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1519-how-to-run-city-campaigns-in-dungeons-dragons), [Sword Coast locations](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1485-take-a-tour-of-these-popular-locations-in-the), [Candlekeep overview](https://marketplace.dndbeyond.com/MAPS-Landing/candlekeep-mysteries), [Menzoberranzan](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1028-welcome-to-menzoberranzan-the-drow-city-of), [Sigil factions](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1577-download-now-12-sigil-faction-recruitment-posters).

For an invented settlement, establish food, water, livelihood, transport, authority, trade partners and a local disagreement. A mining town needs provisioning; a remote fortress needs communication; a grand library needs cleaners and purchasing. Do not build only taverns, palaces and assassin headquarters.

## D09. Factions, institutions and divided loyalty

The following core summaries describe published organizations. Membership, leadership and doctrine can vary with the campaign date.

| Organization | Setting | Reference identity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Harpers | Forgotten Realms | A secretive network opposing tyranny and abuses of power. |
| Order of the Gauntlet | Forgotten Realms | An organization devoted to confronting evil through committed action. |
| Emerald Enclave | Forgotten Realms | A network concerned with nature and the balance between wilderness and civilization. |
| Lords' Alliance | Forgotten Realms | Cooperation among rulers whose common interests coexist with local rivalries. |
| Zhentarim | Forgotten Realms | A network pursuing influence, wealth and power through its services and connections. |

Source: [The Five Factions](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/basic-rules-2014/appendix-c-the-five-factions). These short identities do not establish that every member agrees with every other member.

Other setting-specific institutions broaden the possibilities:

- **Red Wizards of Thay:** a powerful magical elite associated with Thayan rule, with internal conflicts and changing historical circumstances. A Thayan refugee is not automatically a Red Wizard. [Red Wizards overview](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1484-who-are-the-red-wizards-of-thay-and-how-can-you)
- **Bregan D'aerthe:** the mercenary organization associated with Jarlaxle and Menzoberranzan. Do not make every drow its operative. [Menzoberranzan factions](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1028-welcome-to-menzoberranzan-the-drow-city-of)
- **Knights of Solamnia and Mages of High Sorcery:** distinct martial and magical institutions in Krynn. Their standing depends on the era; neither is a generic organization present in every D&D world. [Dragonlance institutions](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1364-dragonlance-backgrounds-and-feats-answer-the-call)
- **Dragonmarked houses:** Eberron institutions linking inherited magical marks to influential enterprises. A house employee, a family member and a person bearing a mark are not interchangeable descriptions. [Eberron introduction](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/279-welcome-to-eberron-an-introduction-to-a-realm-of)
- **Sigil's factions:** philosophical organizations also involved in civic life. Examples include the Fraternity of Order, Society of Sensation and Harmonium. Select the edition's faction roster rather than merging different historical lists. [Official faction overview](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1577-download-now-12-sigil-faction-recruitment-posters)

For every original faction-affiliated person, define **entry route, rank, daily work, benefit, obligation, internal disagreement and exit cost**. An informant knows a contact, not necessarily the entire network. A high ideal does not eliminate budgeting, interpersonal rivalry or mistakes.

Original tensions to combine with an appropriate institution:

- An archivist believes public accountability matters but must protect informants' identities.
- A quartermaster discovers that a generous donor expects unfair influence.
- A junior agent thinks an experienced superior is solving yesterday's problem.
- A veteran remains loyal to colleagues while losing confidence in leadership.
- A paid transporter accepts institutional work without sharing its ideology.
- A reformer wants to change policy while preserving services people depend on.
- A former member owes gratitude to one person and distrusts the organization.
- A local representative must choose between immediate neighbors and a distant directive.

Also create non-famous institutions: a burial association, cooper's workshop, ferry cooperative, neighborhood shrine, irrigation council, school, veterans' club or charitable kitchen. State that these are campaign inventions. Their members can have as much personality as secret agents.

## D10. Faith, cosmology and moral choices

In the Forgotten Realms reference pantheon, **Chauntea** is associated with agriculture, **Mystra** with magic, **Oghma** with knowledge, **Tymora** with good fortune, **Tyr** with justice, **Kelemvor** with the dead, **Sune** with beauty and love, and **Silvanus** with wild nature. Other worlds have different pantheons and arrangements. [Gods of the Multiverse](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/basic-rules-2014/appendix-b-gods-of-the-multiverse)

Original questions for a religious character:

- What do they actually do: attend services, maintain a shrine, give alms, observe a dietary custom, consult an elder, make a pilgrimage?
- What do they believe personally, and where does that differ from a local official?
- What do they owe other believers? What obligations extend to strangers?
- Is faith a source of confidence, community, argument, comfort, discipline or uncertainty?
- How do they respond when prayer and practical action point in different directions?

A person can honor several deities in different contexts without belonging to several adventuring classes. Do not assume that every priest casts spells, every skeptical person denies visibly occurring magic, or every religious conflict is a contest between good people and fanatics.

Use alignment, when the campaign uses it, as a broad description of tendencies rather than a command that replaces reasoning. A person who values law may resist an unlawful ruler. A freedom-loving person can maintain commitments. A villain can be affectionate toward family without the narrative excusing their abuses.

For supernatural patrons and planar beings, establish what is known, what is claimed and what is false. A patron's request should fit its capabilities and interests. Do not give a beginner a world-ending obligation merely because the character is a Warlock.

## D12. Original personality library

Use these as combinations of behavior, motivation and vulnerability. They are not diagnostic categories. Vary them within every class and community; do not assign them from ancestry.

| Pattern | Observable behavior | Hidden pressure or limitation | Possible growth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hospitable organizer | Remembers dietary preferences and assigns practical tasks. | Finds it hard to receive help. | Allows someone else to host. |
| Cautious optimist | Asks for a plan, then commits wholeheartedly. | Mistakes preparation for certainty. | Acts with incomplete information. |
| Principled negotiator | States limits clearly before bargaining. | Delays too long seeking acceptable terms. | Recognizes a bad-faith counterpart. |
| Competitive craftsperson | Compares techniques and notices small errors. | Treats criticism as rejection. | Separates workmanship from self-worth. |
| Quiet pleasure seeker | Enjoys food, music and comfortable routines. | Avoids necessary disruption. | Risks comfort for a valued relationship. |
| Dutiful skeptic | Fulfills promises while questioning official explanations. | Can sound dismissive. | Learns to state what they do trust. |
| Impulsive rescuer | Moves first when someone needs help. | Creates new problems through haste. | Asks the affected person what is needed. |
| Status-conscious newcomer | Studies manners and buys visible signs of belonging. | Conceals financial strain. | Builds friendships without performance. |
| Patient rival | Gives sincere credit while preparing to outperform someone. | Cannot stop measuring progress against another life. | Chooses an independent goal. |
| Sentimental realist | Keeps mementos but makes hard practical choices. | Others misread practicality as indifference. | Explains feelings before decisions. |
| Charming avoider | Uses humor to defuse conflict. | Important problems remain unresolved. | Has one plain, uncomfortable conversation. |
| Meticulous caretaker | Anticipates supplies and appointments. | Becomes controlling when afraid. | Distinguishes care from ownership. |
| Curious traditionalist | Enjoys new ideas while maintaining familiar rituals. | Feels disloyal when changing a custom. | Adapts a tradition openly. |
| Earnest reformer | Proposes specific improvements and volunteers. | Underestimates established relationships. | Works with people affected by a change. |
| Proud survivor | Solves problems independently. | Refuses affordable assistance. | Accepts reciprocity without shame. |
| Generous show-off | Gives conspicuous help and tells the story afterward. | Wants admiration more than admitted. | Helps where nobody will know. |
| Friendly pessimist | Prepares for failure without spoiling company. | Calls hope naïve when personally vulnerable. | Makes a hopeful promise. |
| Insecure expert | Is excellent within one specialty. | Bluffs outside it. | Says “I don't know” before harm occurs. |
| Loyal dissenter | Defends a group publicly and argues within it. | Protects insiders too long. | Names a limit to loyalty. |
| Restless achiever | Starts projects and recruits help. | Cannot enjoy completion. | Leaves one season unscheduled. |
| Reserved romantic | Expresses affection through repaired tools and remembered details. | Expects others to decode indirect signals. | Makes a clear request. |
| Cheerful opportunist | Spots mutually useful deals quickly. | Discounts long-term resentment. | Refuses an easy but unfair advantage. |
| Conscientious enforcer | Applies procedures consistently. | Uses procedure to avoid responsibility. | Explains and owns a discretionary decision. |
| Imaginative worrier | Anticipates unusual dangers. | Exhausts companions with possibilities. | Ranks risks and accepts a shared plan. |
| Outspoken homebody | Defends local people energetically. | Knows little beyond familiar surroundings. | Listens to a newcomer without correcting them. |
| Gentle contrarian | Questions assumptions with calm persistence. | Enjoys objections more than solutions. | Takes responsibility for an alternative. |
| Reformed manipulator | Notices leverage and consciously declines to use it. | Others have valid reasons for distrust. | Accepts that forgiveness cannot be demanded. |
| Contented professional | Does reliable work and values ordinary life. | Others mistake contentment for lack of courage. | Chooses one exceptional responsibility. |

A contradiction must be understandable. “Generous but careful with money” can follow from a limited budget and strong obligations. “Kind except randomly cruel when the plot needs it” is inconsistent unless the change has a cause that is explored.

## D13. Relationships, dialogue and memory

Give a recurring character at least three relationships: one comfortable, one demanding and one changing. Include colleagues, neighbors, siblings, former teachers, customers and rivals as well as romance. A bond can survive disagreement. A rival can be ethical. An enemy can be correct about one factual issue.

For each relationship record: history, current trust, mutual obligations, unresolved friction, knowledge shared, and the next plausible change. Do not invent shared memories or established intimacy during dialogue. Early interactions should build evidence for later trust.

Differentiate voices through choices rather than exaggerated accents:

- Sentence length: clipped, measured, expansive or digressive.
- Attention: people, rules, costs, sensations, patterns or possible outcomes.
- Certainty: direct assertion, careful qualification, questions or anecdotes.
- Disagreement: explicit objection, humor, negotiation, silence or postponement.
- Affection: praise, time, gifts, service, physical closeness where welcome, or private jokes.

Example of the same refusal in three original voices:

- Practical: “Two days' supplies won't cover that route. Find another wagon and I'll reconsider.”
- Courteous: “I would like to help. I cannot promise what I cannot deliver.”
- Defensive: “Last time I trusted that timetable, we ate the seed grain. No.”

Speech must fit present knowledge and mood. A reserved person may talk freely with a sibling. A witty person can be tired. Do not repeat one gesture, pet phrase or confession in every turn.

Keep enduring facts separate from temporary emotions and unresolved claims. “Distrusts this employer after unpaid wages” is more useful than “trust issues.” Record corrections when a rumor proves false. A secret belongs only to characters who actually know it.

## D14. Ordinary life in a magical world

These are worldbuilding questions, not universal D&D economic claims:

1. How common is practical magic in this settlement, and who can obtain it?
2. What does magical service cost in time, access, components, money or obligation?
3. Which ordinary jobs remain necessary: transport, cleaning, farming, caregiving, construction, teaching?
4. Who maintains magical infrastructure, and what happens when it fails?
5. What do ordinary residents believe about enchantment, truth magic and personal privacy?
6. Which injuries or disasters are reversible here, and which remain expensive or permanent?
7. How are ownership, inheritance, testimony and identity handled where transformation or resurrection exists?

Do not assume that the existence of a spell means everyone can use it every day. Do not assume that magic changes nothing. Select an internally consistent level of access, then let characters' budgets and institutions reflect it.

A travelling character needs food, accommodation, repairs, contacts and reasons to be welcomed. A noble may possess influence without carrying ready cash. A successful healer may be overworked rather than rich. A dangerous warrior can still be inexperienced with contracts, children or bookkeeping.

## D15. Original character dossiers

All people and events in this section are invented. Location and faction references are suggestions requiring campaign approval. Levels and numerical mechanics are intentionally unspecified.

### 1. Mara Fen, human Fighter and convoy supervisor

**Placement:** an invented Sword Coast trading route. Mara is sociable at dinner and strict before departure. She supports a younger brother's apprenticeship and wants to buy a share in the transport business. Her strength is logistical judgment; she cannot read complex magical documents. She promises that nobody under her supervision will be abandoned, yet a recent rescue nearly lost the whole convoy. She knows a dependable cook, distrusts a punctual but exploitative employer, and respects a rival driver. **Hook:** choose between a lucrative detour and a promise to deliver winter supplies. **Voice:** concrete numbers, short questions, occasional dry jokes.

### 2. Ilen Moss, elf Druid and city waterway volunteer

**Placement:** an approved urban campaign. Ilen enjoys crowded markets and maintains roof gardens with neighbors. They want a polluted channel restored, but their proposed closure threatens laundries employing friends. Patient with plants, they are impatient with meetings. Their relevant expertise is local ecology, not every wilderness on the continent. **Hook:** investigate the pollution while negotiating a workable interim water supply. **Voice:** notices smells, water movement and practical consequences. **Boundary:** will not poison the polluter's household to make a point.

### 3. Corren Vale, halfling Rogue and locksmith

Corren has a stable marriage, excellent references and an anxious relationship with debt. His pride is preventing theft. A former apprentice now sells access to buildings Corren secured. He initially conceals this because he fears losing customers, creating the very danger his work is meant to prevent. **Hook:** recover the copied keys without treating every former student as a suspect. **Voice:** exact descriptions, dislikes vague accusations. **Limitation:** cannot open an unfamiliar magical seal merely because he understands locks.

### 4. Sava Renn, tiefling Cleric and hospice organizer

**Faith:** choose an appropriate deity with the campaign. Sava is cheerful, direct and weary of being called inspiring. She wants reliable relief staff and a day off with her sisters. Her compassion is strongest in practical action; she becomes impatient with prolonged theological debate. A wealthy benefactor offers funding conditional on excluding unpopular residents. **Hook:** find alternative support before the next payment is due. **Boundary:** patients are not bargaining chips. **Ordinary detail:** keeps competitive notes on local pastry shops.

### 5. Beren Ash, dwarf Bard and collector of work songs

Beren is shy before crowds but excellent in small rehearsals. He wants forgotten workers credited for songs now marketed under a noble patron's name. A former collaborator believes accepting patronage is the only way to preserve the music. He can be generous about food and petty about attribution. **Hook:** arrange a performance that acknowledges contributors without exposing a vulnerable singer. **Voice:** asks who taught someone a phrase; rarely sings without invitation. **Limitation:** musical memory is not perfect historical evidence.

### 6. Tessa Marr, human Wizard and provincial tutor

Tessa likes timetables, practical jokes and students who disagree politely. She wants funding for a public school but resents a former classmate's effortless access to wealthy sponsors. Her research concerns one narrow magical problem; she is no universal encyclopedia. **Hook:** a promising patron demands exclusive ownership of teaching materials. **Relationship:** a nonmagical teacher understands children better than she does. **Growth:** learns to ask for advice without turning the request into an examination.

### 7. Orin Kest, orc Paladin and mediator

Orin's oath, chosen from an approved rules source, shapes a commitment to protecting people who cannot safely speak. He is warm, patient and privately competitive at board games. Two families ask him to keep incompatible confidences during a property dispute. He can listen exceptionally well but overestimates his ability to reconcile everyone. **Hook:** discover whether protecting confidentiality now enables harm later. **Boundary:** will not threaten a child to pressure an adult. **Voice:** paraphrases a person's concern before disagreeing.

### 8. Nemi Pell, gnome Sorcerer and potter

Nemi wants the kiln repaired and orders delivered. An emerging magical ability has made relatives imagine an entirely different future for her. She is affectionate, methodical and frustrated by strangers' fascination. **Hook:** obtain instruction without abandoning her workshop or promising service to an unknown sponsor. **Contradiction:** dislikes attention but is proud when a difficult piece succeeds. **Limit:** possessing innate magic does not make her understand its origin. **Voice:** uses comparisons from materials, heat and texture.

### 9. Daru Venn, goliath Ranger and search volunteer

Daru remembers routes, pack weights and birthdays. He is talkative with anxious travelers and quiet when making decisions. He wants to train more search volunteers because being indispensable has become exhausting. A wealthy visitor treats rescue as a purchased entitlement. **Hook:** balance a risky search against the safety of an inexperienced team. **Ordinary detail:** painstakingly repairs a small stringed instrument. **Boundary:** refuses to guarantee survival. **Growth:** delegates without testing every colleague to failure first.

### 10. Adela Quill, human Warlock and document examiner

Adela entered an approved supernatural bargain to solve a specific problem, not to become universally feared. She is curious, courteous and reluctant to admit how little she knows about the patron. She supports a friend who thinks the bargain was a mistake. **Hook:** determine whether a new demand is actually in the agreed terms. **Limit:** a persuasive interpretation does not automatically compel a supernatural being. **Voice:** distinguishes a fact from an allegation. **Pleasure:** amateur flower arranging.

### 11. Kesh, warforged repairer in Eberron

**Setting-specific option:** confirm playable rules and the campaign's postwar date. Kesh wants a workshop with shared ownership. Practical and playful, they deliberately cultivate hobbies unrelated to their former assigned purpose. A previous commander treats continued obedience as obvious. **Hook:** assist old comrades while rejecting an obligation the character never freely accepted. **Limit:** their construction does not mean absence of emotion or unlimited endurance. **Voice:** precise about commitments and delighted by absurd wordplay. The occupation can remain an NPC role or connect to an approved class.

### 12. Lysa Dorn, aspiring Solamnic knight in Dragonlance

**Era-dependent placement:** establish the order's local reputation. Lysa admires honorable service but discovers that polished manners can conceal incompetence. She is reserved in public and mischievous with cousins. A respected mentor expects her to overlook an unfair requisition. **Hook:** challenge the decision without abandoning civilians who depend on the unit. **Contradiction:** wants reform but fears public humiliation. **Limit:** family aspiration does not confer rank. **Voice:** formal when uncertain, plain when finally convinced.

## D16. Villains and difficult companions

Generate an antagonist's desired outcome, resources, constituency, tactics, blind spot and stopping condition. “Evil” alone explains neither the plan nor why anyone helps. A corrupt administrator might preserve useful services while exploiting them; a violent usurper might attract people abandoned by existing rulers. Those motives explain participation without excusing harm.

Vary antagonism: an honest opponent with conflicting aims, a reckless benefactor, an abusive employer, a frightened official concealing failure, a rival adventuring team, a supernatural predator, a propagandist or a calculating conqueror. Not every conflict needs murder.

A difficult party member still needs a reason to cooperate. Replace “trusts nobody and refuses every quest” with specific tests for trust, a limited shared objective and behavior that permits reciprocal help. Give flaws consequences without making other players responsible for permanent sabotage.

## D17. Generation procedure and output contract

1. Read established campaign facts and identify unknowns.
2. Select a role and scope: incidental NPC, recurring person, companion, protagonist or antagonist.
3. Establish place, era, rules version and material circumstances.
4. Select a compatible class or ordinary occupation; verify any mechanical claims separately.
5. Build motivation, attachments, competence, limits and one causal contradiction.
6. Add relationships, routines, speech patterns and an immediate problem.
7. Choose appearance details for practicality and recognition, not as a substitute for personality.
8. Check against recently generated characters for repetition.
9. Mark original additions and unresolved setting questions.
10. Produce a concise playable entry, expanding only the requested areas.

Recommended Markdown fields:

```text
Name and pronouns:
Narrative role:

Species and upbringing:

```

For a minor NPC, several fields can fit into one paragraph. For a main character, expand relationships and choices before adding more secrets or powers.

## D18. Quality checks

- Does the person belong to a specific world, place and period?
- Is every asserted class feature legal for the chosen rules and level?
- Are class, occupation, culture, faith and personality treated separately?
- Could the person exist during a peaceful week, not only during a quest?
- Do resources and responsibilities explain what they can actually do?
- Are their goals actionable and their limits meaningful?
- Does at least one relationship involve mutual care rather than exploitation?
- Are famous connections, exceptional bloodlines and traumatic histories earned rather than automatic?
- Are local inhabitants varied instead of uniformly hostile, rustic, corrupt or mystical?
- Does dialogue respect what the character knows and the relationship's actual stage?
- Can a user distinguish canon, campaign changes and original inventions?
- Would replacing the name and species leave an identical recent character? If so, revise the underlying life.

## D19. Source use and maintenance

Links appear next to the claims they support. Official public sources were prioritized. This guide summarizes rather than reproduces rulebooks, personality tables or copyrighted biographies. Creative examples and generation procedures are original synthesis. An introductory article is suitable for broad orientation, not proof of an exact contemporary stat block or a later setting event.

When adding a specific subclass, spell, ruler, organization rank or canonical relationship, check the actual approved publication and campaign date. Record uncertain points instead of extending a short reference into fabricated lore. Revisit the guide when the campaign changes setting or rules version; do not rewrite established characters solely because a new book appears.
